## <i class="fas fa-feather"></i> Why Tcl/Tk?

In this first book, we take a brief look at **Tcl/Tk**, a simple yet versatile general-purpose interpreted language.

Tcl is widely used in electronics, particularly in environments involving **FPGAs**, and it also comes with **Tk**, its graphical toolkit.

{{% notice style="tip" title="Tcl + Tk" icon="lightbulb" %}}
Tcl is already interesting on its own, but **Tk makes it easy to create graphical interfaces**, turning the combination into an especially appealing tool for electronics projects.
{{% /notice %}}

---

## <i class="fas fa-window-maximize"></i> More than hardware control

One of the advantages of Tcl/Tk is that what you learn is not limited to your electronics projects.

You can also use it to create your own:

- desktop applications;
- graphical interfaces for controlling hardware;
- support tools for your projects;
- GUI applications that you can adapt to your own needs.

In other words, the same environment you use to communicate with an electronics project can also be used to build the application that controls it.
